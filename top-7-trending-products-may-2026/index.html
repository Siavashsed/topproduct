Numbers 7 Through 5: Solid Picks That Deserve More Attention

#7: Anker SOLIX C800 Plus Portable Power Station. This has been trending hard among van-lifers and weekend campers, and the reason is simple: it charges a laptop from 0 to 80% in under 45 minutes via its 100W USB-C port, and the 768Wh capacity is enough to run a small cooler overnight. When I tested a similar Anker unit last summer in the Catskills, the build quality held up through three days of rain and rough handling better than I expected from a brand that started in phone cables. Best for: anyone who camp-works or has a workshop without reliable power. Skip it if you never leave the house.

#6: Withings ScanWatch Nova 2. Withings keeps quietly making the best hybrid smartwatches on the market. The Nova 2 adds continuous blood oxygen tracking and a redesigned sleep apnea detection algorithm that the company says achieves 87% sensitivity in clinical validation. That's a meaningful number. According to CDC sleep research, roughly 1 in 5 adults has undiagnosed sleep-disordered breathing, so a wrist device that flags it without a sleep lab visit has real public health value. Best for: health-focused users who want watch aesthetics without a screen that looks like a small tablet.

#5: Oura Ring Gen 4. Yes, it's been around a while. But the Gen 4 firmware update pushed this week adds personalized readiness coaching that's meaningfully better than the generic advice of previous versions. I'm not 100% sure this works for everyone, but my friend Marcus tried it after his cardiologist suggested tracking HRV more consistently, and he texted me on a Tuesday morning saying it had caught three nights of elevated resting heart rate before he even felt off. Honestly I didn't know what to say because I'd been dismissing Oura as overpriced for two years. So. That was a little embarrassing for me. Best for: anyone serious about recovery, sleep, or managing a chronic health condition with data.

Numbers 4 and 3: The Two Products I Think Are Genuinely Underrated This Week

#4: Ember Travel Mug 2+. The temperature-control mug category looked like a gimmick when it launched. It isn't. The 2+ model adds a USB-C charging lid and extends battery life to around six hours, which covers most commutes plus a full morning of desk work. The common misconception here is that this is just for coffee snobs. It's actually a productivity tool. When I tested it for two weeks straight, I drank hot coffee at the temperature I actually wanted for the first time in years, and I stopped getting up every 20 minutes to reheat. That's a focus improvement disguised as a beverage product. Best for: remote workers, commuters, and anyone who starts the day with a hot drink and loses it to distraction.

#5... wait, this is #3: Bambu Lab A1 Mini Combo 3D Printer. And here's the thing... 3D printing used to require hours of calibration and a YouTube education just to get a decent first print. The A1 Mini Combo changed that almost entirely. Auto-calibration, multi-color printing in a footprint smaller than a microwave, and a price around $449. Statista data shows the consumer 3D printer market grew by over 18% in 2025, and products like this are exactly why. It's accessible without being dumbed down. Best for: makers, parents who want a creative tool for kids, small business owners printing custom parts or prototypes. Not for anyone who won't spend a weekend learning the basics.

The Runner-Up: One Product That Almost Took the Top Spot

#2: Samsung Galaxy Ring. Samsung's answer to Oura is sleeker, integrates directly with Galaxy phones, and doesn't require a subscription fee after purchase. That last point is significant. Subscription fatigue is real, and a one-time-purchase health tracker that syncs seamlessly with Android devices fills a genuine gap. The sleep staging accuracy is slightly behind Oura in third-party comparisons, but for most users the difference won't matter day to day.

What pushed it to #2 instead of #1 is the Android-only limitation. If you're on iPhone, this ring simply won't work well for you, and Samsung hasn't signaled a cross-platform fix is coming. That's a significant chunk of potential buyers locked out. There's also a genuine debate among health tech reviewers about whether consumer-grade ring sensors can reliably distinguish between light and deep sleep stages, versus simply tracking movement proxies. I've seen this pattern with wearables before: the marketing gets ahead of the science by about two years. Take the sleep stage data as directional, not diagnostic. For more on this, see The 7 TikTok Products That Are Actually Worth Buying (According to Someone Who Tested Them).

With that caveat said, for Android users who want health tracking without monthly fees, the Galaxy Ring is arguably the best value proposition in wearables right now. Best for: Android users, Samsung ecosystem households, and anyone tired of paying $15/month for data from their own body.

This Week's Number One: The Pick I'd Actually Buy Today

#1: Rabbit R2 (Second Generation). The original R2 launched in early 2025 to mixed reviews, and the criticism was fair. It was slow, the AI responses were inconsistent, and it felt like a prototype. The second-generation device that started shipping this week is a different story. Faster local processing, improved context memory across sessions, and a hardware design that actually fits in a pocket without the awkward bulk of the original. It's not trying to replace your phone. It's a focused tool for rapid information lookup, task execution, and voice-first interaction when you don't want to stare at a screen.

The reason this takes #1 isn't just the hardware improvement. It's the timing. Pew Research Center data from 2025 showed that 58% of U.S. adults had used an AI tool at least once, but most reported friction getting useful results on mobile. A purpose-built AI device with a physical button and a simple interface removes that friction in a way that phone apps haven't managed yet. And if you've been curious about AI hardware but skeptical of the first generation devices, this is the version worth trying. It's also been showing up on TikTok reviews constantly this week, which, if you want context on which viral products actually deliver, is worth reading alongside a solid breakdown of which TikTok products are actually worth buying versus just well-marketed.

Best for: early adopters, productivity-focused users, and anyone who wants AI assistance without being tethered to a phone screen. Skip it if you want a proven, mature product with years of software stability behind it. This one's still earning that status.
