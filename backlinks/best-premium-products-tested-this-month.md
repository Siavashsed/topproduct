## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Are premium products actually worth the extra money?

Sometimes, but far less often than the price tag suggests. I'm Jamie Cole, I review products for a living, and I just spent four weeks testing seven premium products side by side in my own apartment. Only two of them justified what they cost.

Here's the framework I use now.

1. Measure use, not features. I log every product in a spreadsheet: how often I actually reach for it, and whether I'd buy it again at full price. The Sony WH-1000XM6 topped my list this month for a boring reason. I used it every day without thinking about it. Cost per use drops fast once something becomes part of your routine.

2. Separate "I love it" from "it's worth it." A $1,800 chair was the worst value of the seven, and I still kind of love it. Both things are true. Enjoyment is real, but if a cheaper option gives you 90 percent of the benefit, the premium is buying the last 10 percent. Decide whether that last 10 percent matters to you.

3. Watch for products that add stress. On day nine I caught myself pulling off a sleep-tracking ring at 2 a.m. because I was anxious about my own sleep score. If a product gives you something new to worry about, count that as part of the price.

4. Pay more for the things you use for hours. Headphones, a mattress and the tools you work with every day are where premium quality most often pays off. Things you use occasionally rarely earn it.
