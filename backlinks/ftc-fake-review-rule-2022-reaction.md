## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How can I tell if online product reviews are fake, and will the FTC's fake review rule actually help?

I review products for a living, and I still got fooled. Last March I bought a $64 robot vacuum with 4.7 stars across roughly 3,100 reviews. The dustbin never latched. When I went back and read more carefully, the warning signs were all there.

Here's what I check now:

1. Sort by newest. Fake reviews often arrive in bursts, dozens of five-stars within a few days, followed by quiet.

2. Read the three-star reviews first. People who give three stars tend to describe real pros and cons. Five-star and one-star reviews are where most manipulation lives.

3. Look for specifics. Real buyers mention battery life after a month, a latch that broke, how it handled pet hair. Fake ones say 'great product, highly recommend, fast shipping.'

4. Check reviewer profiles. A reviewer who posted twenty five-star reviews in one week across unrelated product categories is a red flag.

5. Watch for listing merges. Sellers sometimes attach a new product to an old listing with thousands of reviews for a different item.

6. Cross-check outside the retailer. Independent testing sites, forums, and YouTube teardown videos are harder to game at scale.

As for the FTC: on October 20, 2022, it started the formal process of writing a rule specifically targeting fake reviews. That's a meaningful signal, but a rule in development doesn't change what you'll see on product pages this holiday season. Enforcement takes time, and sellers who game ratings won't stop overnight.
