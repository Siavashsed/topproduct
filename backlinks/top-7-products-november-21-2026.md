## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How can I tell if a product is actually worth buying instead of just well marketed?

I'm Jamie Cole, and I review products for a living. Here is what I've learned the hard way: marketing and quality are almost unrelated, and the label on the box is the least reliable thing about a product.

I learned that last week. My $19 power bank died at 11% charge in the Denver airport with a three-hour delay ahead of me. The packaging promised a capacity the battery clearly didn't have.

Here is the checklist I use now.

1. Look for the catch. Every product has a downside. If reviews only praise something, they're probably ads. I always want to know who it's wrong for and when it fails.

2. Check repairability and parts availability. A product you can open, fix, and upgrade will outlast one that's sealed. This is why the Framework Laptop 13 has become my top recommendation. You replace a worn battery or a broken port instead of the whole machine.

3. Distrust vague specs. Claims like 'fast charging' or 'up to 20,000 mAh' with no test conditions are red flags. Real numbers come with details.

4. Read the one- and two-star reviews. They're where the long-term failures show up, usually six months in.

5. Study the return and warranty terms. A company that makes returns painful doesn't trust its own product.

6. Wait for independent testing when you can. Early hype fades quickly, and failures don't.

None of this requires technical expertise. It requires patience and a willingness to ignore the loudest voice in the room.
