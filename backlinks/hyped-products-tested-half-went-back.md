## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Are viral products like the Dyson Airwrap, Ninja Creami and Stanley Quencher actually worth the hype?

Short answer: most of them are good products. Whether they're worth it depends on whether they solve a problem you have every day, and that's harder to judge than it sounds.

I review products for a living. Last year I bought eleven of the most hyped ones at once, including the three you mentioned. I didn't write first impressions. I kept a spreadsheet for 90 days and logged what I actually reached for, what broke and what I stopped using. About half went back.

Here's what I learned:

1. The first two weeks are almost useless for judging. Everything feels great while it's new. The honest data shows up after the novelty wears off, when you either grab the thing without thinking or walk past it.

2. Fine is the most common result. Very few hyped products are bad. The problem is paying a premium for something that, in your real routine, does no more than a cheaper alternative.

3. Hype tracks how good a product looks on camera, not how often you'll use it. Color drops, limited editions and unboxing appeal are built for social feeds. They tell you nothing about month three.

4. The keepers solve one specific problem you hit again and again. If you can't name that problem in a sentence, you have your answer.

My test before buying anything viral: picture the exact moment in your week when you'd use it and check the return window. Then decide before the window closes, based on how much you used it, not how you feel about it. If you can't picture that moment, wait a month. The urge usually fades.
