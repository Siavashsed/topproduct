**Title:** Anyone else skip the star average and go straight to the three-star reviews?

I got burned in March 2024 on a $79 handheld steam cleaner. It had a 4.8 rating from about 2,300 reviews, and it leaked from the base within eleven days. When I went back and actually read through the reviews, a big chunk of the five-star ones looked off. They were short, vague, and often posted in bursts within a few days of each other.

So now I do this instead of trusting the average:

1. Read the three-star reviews first. These people usually liked something and can explain exactly what bugged them.
2. Then read the one-star reviews, but only the ones that name a specific failure, like 'the seal cracked at week two' or 'the battery stopped holding a charge'. Ignore the angry rants with no detail.
3. Check whether the same complaint shows up more than once. If three strangers describe the same leak, that's your answer.
4. Look at review dates. A pile of glowing reviews in one week is a red flag.

It takes about five minutes and has saved me from a few bad purchases since. Curious what other people look for. Do you have a tell that a review is fake?

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How can you tell if product reviews on Amazon are fake or unreliable?

The short version: stop relying on the star average and read the middle of the distribution instead.

I review products for a living, and the most useful habit I've built is to read the three-star reviews first. People who give three stars usually liked part of the product and can explain clearly what fell short, so they have little motive to exaggerate in either direction.

Next, read the one-star reviews, but only the ones that name a specific failure. 'It leaked from the base after ten days' is useful. 'Total garbage, don't buy' is not. What you're looking for is the same specific complaint appearing from multiple unrelated people. Three strangers describing the same cracked seal is strong evidence.

Then look at the five-star reviews with a skeptical eye. Common warning signs:

- Very short, generic praise like 'Great product, love it!'
- Reviews that describe the category rather than the actual item
- Many reviews posted within a few days of each other
- Reviewers whose history is nothing but five-star ratings across unrelated products
- Language that sounds like it was lifted from the product listing

I learned this the hard way in March 2024. I bought a $79 handheld steam cleaner rated 4.8 from roughly 2,300 reviews, and it leaked within eleven days. When I went back, a surprising number of those glowing reviews fit the patterns above.

None of this requires special tools. It takes about five minutes per purchase, and it has saved me far more money than any coupon. I wrote up the full process, with examples, here if you want more detail: https://siavashsed.github.io/topproduct/read-product-reviews-spot-fake-ones/

The main point is simple: specific, repeated detail is hard to fake, while a high average is easy to manufacture.
