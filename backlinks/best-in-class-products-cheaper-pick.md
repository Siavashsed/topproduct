## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Are expensive noise-cancelling headphones really worth it compared to a $100 pair?

It depends on what you do with them, but for many people the answer is less obvious than the price gap suggests.

I tested this on a 6 a.m. flight out of Denver in March. On the way out I wore Sony WH-1000XM5s, which run about $400. On the return I wore a borrowed $99 pair. I assumed the cheaper ones would feel flimsy and let the engine noise through. They didn't. The noise cancelling was strong enough that I stopped noticing the drone after about ten minutes, and the fit stayed comfortable for hours.

Where the expensive pair did pull ahead was in the details: more refined noise cancelling in changing environments like a busy gate, noticeably better microphone quality on calls, and a more polished feel overall. If you take a lot of calls, travel constantly, or care about those finer points, the premium price can be justified.

If you mostly want quiet for flights, commutes and focus work, a $100 pair gets you most of the benefit. My rough rule is to ask what problem the extra $300 solves for you specifically. If you can name it, buy the better pair. If you can't, you're likely paying for a brand and a spot on the top of the lists.

A few practical tips: buy from a retailer with an easy return window, test them in the environment where you'll actually use them, and give them a week before deciding. Fit and comfort matter more than spec sheets.
