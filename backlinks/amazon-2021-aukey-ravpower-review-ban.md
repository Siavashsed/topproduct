## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why did Amazon ban Aukey and RavPower, and can I still trust Amazon reviews?

Amazon removed Aukey, RavPower, Mpow and hundreds of other electronics sellers in 2021 for breaking its review policies. The core issue was incentivized reviews: offering buyers money, usually a gift card or a refund, in exchange for positive ratings.

I saw this firsthand. In March 2021 I bought a RavPower 26,800mAh power bank for $59.99. Under the foam was a small orange card offering a $35 Amazon gift card for leaving a review. That's more than half the price of the product, and it isn't hard to guess what kind of review the money was meant to buy.

The scheme worked because these reviews came from verified purchases: real buyers, real orders, real photos. The usual advice to ignore unverified reviews didn't help, because the manipulation happened inside the box, where Amazon couldn't see it. A database leak reported in May 2021 also exposed sellers arranging reviews directly with reviewers.

Can you still trust Amazon reviews? Partly, if you read them differently:

- Read the 3-star reviews first. They tend to be the most honest about tradeoffs.
- Check the dates. A burst of 5-star reviews in a short window is a warning sign.
- Search the reviews for words like 'card', 'refund' or 'gift'. Buyers sometimes mention the insert.
- Compare against independent testing from reviewers who buy their own units.
- Be wary of brand names that look randomly generated, since banned sellers often relaunch under new ones.

To be fair, many of the banned products weren't bad. Plenty worked fine. The problem was that the ratings no longer told you how they compared to the alternatives.
