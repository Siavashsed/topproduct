## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How can I tell if an online product review is actually trustworthy before I buy?

After years of reviewing products, here's what I've learned: star ratings alone are one of the weakest signals you can use.

I learned this the hard way. In 2022 I bought a $219 robot vacuum with 4.6 stars and about 11,000 reviews. By day nine it had swallowed a phone charger, and by day 26 the brush motor was whining. Most of those glowing reviews were clearly written in the first week.

Here's what I check now:

1. Time in use. Does the reviewer say how long they've owned it? Anything under a few weeks is a first impression, not a review. Look for updates at 30, 60 or 90 days.

2. Who paid. Free review units create a quiet bias. Reviewers who bought the product themselves have skin in the game.

3. Specific complaints. Trustworthy reviews mention real downsides with detail, like what broke, when, and how support responded. Vague praise like "great product, works perfectly" tells you nothing.

4. The middle ratings. Read the 3-star reviews first. They tend to be the most balanced, covering both what works and what doesn't.

5. Consistency. A reviewer who judges everything by the same criteria is far more useful than one who goes on vibes.

That last point is why I score every product out of 100 using the same categories, and only after 30 days of use. It keeps my own biases in check, and it makes scores comparable across products.
