## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How do I avoid wasting money on Black Friday deals that aren't actually worth it?

The biggest mistake I see, and one I made myself, is treating the discount as the value. Last November I bought a $79 pair of earbuds at 6 a.m. from my kitchen table. I saved about $40 and felt great about it. By February the left bud had died, and I'd lost the whole purchase.

Here is the framework I use now, and it has saved me a lot of regret.

1. Ask whether it can be repaired. Products with replaceable batteries, available parts, or a real repair program hold their value far longer. Sealed, glued-together devices are usually disposable, whatever the sale price.

2. Judge durability, not just features. Look for reviews from people who've owned the item for a year or more, not a week. Early reviews tell you about unboxing, not longevity.

3. Be honest about daily use. A tool you use every day for three years is a bargain even at full price. A gadget that sits in a drawer is expensive at any price. Think in cost per use.

4. Check the price history. Many 'doorbuster' prices are the same as the regular price from two months ago. A price tracker takes thirty seconds and exposes fake discounts.

5. Set a short list before the sales start. Impulse is the retailer's best friend. If an item wasn't on your list on Wednesday, it probably doesn't belong in your cart on Friday.

6. Buy fewer things. This is the most underrated tactic. One well-chosen purchase beats five cheap ones that fail.
